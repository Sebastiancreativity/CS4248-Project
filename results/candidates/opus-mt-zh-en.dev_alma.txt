America's “mistakes” have hampered the development of new coronary drugs.
An expert from the United Kingdom ' s new crown drug research and development project said that global progress in the development of new crown drugs had been hampered by the “anything you want” testing of drugs by United States researchers.
To date, 12,000 patients from hundreds of hospitals have joined the British rehabilitation pilot programme, which identifies a new drug that could be used to treat the new crown and notes that the two other drugs previously considered available to treat the disease are ineffective.
However, since the United States medical system does not have a similar rehabilitation programme, other effective treatment methods that would have been approved and widely used have yet to be assessed.
In particular, blood plasma during the recovery period (i.e. plasma taken from patients recovering from the new crown) contains the antibodies of the new coronary virus, which are still subject to large-scale clinical random trials.
Professor Martin Landray, one of the initiators of the rehabilitation programme and expert in large-scale drug clinical trials, said: “In the United States, thousands of people have been treated with plasma during the recovery period without clinical random trials.”
Landray added: “They only provide individuals with a recovery period plasma, but they do not know if it is effective. Although they have already provided a large number of people with a recovery period plasma, it is not clear whether the plasma is effective, harmful to the human body, and has side effects.”
No large-scale clinical random trials were conducted in the United States, resulting in the provision of bad drugs
Clinical random drug trials are the gold standard for determining the efficacy of drugs.
By experimenting, unconscious prejudices that may affect clinical doctor ' s judgement can be eliminated.
In clinical random drug trials, the subjects were divided into treatment and placebo groups.
No one knows what kind of drug the subject accepted.
The test results are then compared to determine which agent has therapeutic effects.
In this way, the rehabilitation programme has demonstrated that steroid-based semison can reduce by one third the death rate of new coronary patients who are treated with a respirator.
The plan also shows that the highly publicized drugs of hydrazine and co-therapy in Lopinave and Litonawe are not effective for patients.
Most importantly, the trials involved a large number of patients, but none of the hospitals had enough patients for their research.
The key advantage of the United Kingdom is its centralized national health-care system.
By contrast, the health-care system in the United States is not perfect, and its only result is to point out that the expensive Radesiwe does not speed up the recovery of the new coronary.
Landray added: “Small drug trials involving dozens or hundreds of patients are under way in the United States, but they have not yielded substantial results.
They did not carry out large-scale clinical random trials and eventually provided bad drugs, which made both the medical effects and the patient's prognosis poor.”
According to Landray, many doctors believe that plasma during the recovery period can play an important role in the treatment of new coronary patients, but this is not the case.
And he added, "The United States has introduced a recovery plasma therapy to a large number of patients without clinical random trials. We have achieved a great deal in comparison with the United States."
Another founder of the rehabilitation programme, Professor Peter Horby, an infectious disease expert, said:
“It has been reported that 20,000 patients in a queue were treated for the recovery period, but they were not clinical random trials. If they were also tested clinically randomly, we would now also know the effects of the recovery period.
Horby added that clinical random tests for the recovery period of plasma had been initiated by the British rehabilitation trial programme, but the results were not expected until the end of the year.

The space engineer stated that the rock donated by the London Museum of Natural History would be used to calibrate robotic detectors and begin to look for signs of life on Mars after the Imposition probe had landed.
Some of the Mars meteorites we have are very fragile, but we chose this one because it's as hard as old boots.
“When you open the instrument and calibrate it before the study is done, you will calibrate it using materials similar to the unknown that will be studied.
So, what's better for studying the rocks on Mars than a rock from Mars?" Professor Caroline Smith, the head of the Museum of Natural History, says.
Smith was also a member of the Mars 2020 scientific team, adding that scientists believed that the rocks they brought back came from Mars.
"The tiny bubbles in the meteorite are exactly the same composition of the atmosphere of Mars, so we're sure that this rock comes from there."
It is believed that this Martian meteorite was created about 600,000 to 700,000 years ago on the impact of an asteroid or comet on Mars, with debris scattered into space.
One of them swept through the solar system and eventually landed on Earth.
The meteor, now known as SAU 008, was found in Oman in 1999 and has been in the custody of the Museum of Natural History ever since.
A high-precision laser launcher called Sherloc, which will be used to decipher the chemical composition of Mars rocks and to determine whether they contain organic substances that may indicate that life has existed or still exists on Mars, is part of the instrument installed on the Fortune detector.
Adds a SAU 008 to ensure that the operation is done with maximum accuracy.
Smith adds: "We have chosen this rock in particular because it fits chemically and it's a very hard rock."
"Some of the Mars meteorites we found were fragile, but they were as hard as old boots."
After finding the research-valued rocks, the Fortune will store them on the surface of Mars.
The rocks will then be sent back to Mars for robotic recovery and for research and analysis from space to Earth.

The Prime Minister of India will attend the temple foundation ceremony at the disputed site.
New Delhi (UNA) - According to the Trust, which oversees the construction of temples, the Prime Minister of India, Narendra Modi, plans to attend next month's founding ceremony of the Hindu temple at the disputed site in northern India, which was originally built as a 16th century mosque and razed to the ground by Hindu fanatics in 1992.
The ceremony was scheduled to take place on 5 August, which the organizers described as the day for Hindus, but was also the first anniversary of the removal by the Indian Parliament of its sole Muslim-majority semi-autonomous state of Jammu and Kashmir.
Neither supporters nor opponents of the Hindu Nationalist Hindu People ' s Party (BJP), of which Modi is a member, could miss this symbolic day, in its manifesto, in which the party committed itself for decades to the right to self-government in Kashmir and to the construction of a temple on the site of the Mughal-era mosque for Hindu God Ram, which is considered by believers to be the birthplace of Ram.
In view of the fact that the new coronary virus is still spreading throughout India, which is reported to be the third most infected country in the world, the World Hindu Assembly (Vishwa Hidu Parishad), an ally of the Hindu People ' s Party, stated that the ceremony would be broadcast live on national television, limiting the number of people and viewers attending the ceremony.
The spokesperson for the organization, Vinod Bansal, stated at a press conference on Saturday that the temple would be the “centre of timeless social harmony, national unity and integration, awakening Hindu identity”.
Following the overwhelming victory of the Indian People ' s Party in the elections, a century-long dispute over the site was resolved last year.
In November, the Supreme Court ruled in favour of the Temple Trust, stating that five acres would be allocated to the Muslim petitioners elsewhere.
Hindu fanatics have long claimed that the Mughal Muslim aggressors built a mosque on the existing temples in the ancient city of Ayodiyah.
In 1992, riots following the demolition of the mosque in December triggered communal violence in which some 2,000 people, mostly Muslims, were killed.
Meanwhile, the Special Court is still seized of the demolition of mosques.
An architect from the hometown of Modi, Ahmedabad, Gujarat, proposed the construction of a temple with a tower sandstone structure at an altitude of 161 feet (49 metres) with five domes.
